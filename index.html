AI cost does not grow in step with the value it creates

A prototype that costs a few dollars a day can cost tens of thousands a month once hundreds of people use it or an autonomous agent is switched on. The reason is structural: every step of an AI agent re-sends everything that came before it. Move the levers to see how quickly the bill diverges, and what changes it.

Tokens are not priced equally

Two facts change how leaders should read "tokens saved". Repeated text can be re-read at a fraction of the price, and the words the model writes or thinks cost several times more than the words it reads. Reducing volume matters, but arranging and limiting it matters more.

What this means for the business

  • Cache-friendly design pays. Text that stays the same between calls can be re-read at about a tenth of the price.
  • Compression can backfire. Rewriting text that was already cached turns cheap reads into full-price writes. Compress only the newest content.
  • Fewer steps beat cheaper steps. An agent that finishes in 3 turns instead of 4 saves more than a small per-call discount.
  • Non-urgent work is half price. Bulk and overnight jobs belong in the batch lane.
  • Thinking is billed as output. Reasoning-heavy models can multiply cost 3 to 8 times unless effort is capped where it is not needed.
